No.

I get asked this every week, usually by a doctor who has just passed their fellowship exams and assumed the restriction on where they can bill Medicare goes with them. It does not. Fellowship and the moratorium are governed by different sections of the Health Insurance Act, and passing one test does not satisfy the other.

## Why the confusion happens

Fellowship gets you specialist registration in general practice. Specialist registration satisfies **section 19AA**, which is the rule about *what* you can bill. [VERIFY: fellowship leads to specialist registration in general practice, and specialist registration satisfies 19AA]

The moratorium sits in **section 19AB**, the rule about *where* you can bill. Section 19AB applies to you because of where you obtained your primary medical degree, or because you studied in Australia or New Zealand as a temporary resident. Nothing you do later changes where you graduated. So the moratorium keeps running regardless of fellowship. [VERIFY: the 19AB page's "who the restrictions apply to" criteria]

The Department's own 19AA page makes the point directly. A temporary-resident international medical graduate is subject to section 19AB indefinitely, "whether you hold specialist registration or not". [VERIFY: exact wording on the 19AA page under "Temporary residents"]

## What fellowship does change

It is not nothing. With fellowship you:

- satisfy section 19AA, which matters the moment you become a permanent resident, because without specialist registration or an approved programme place a permanent resident cannot bill Medicare for private patients at all [VERIFY: paraphrase of the 19AA page]
- gain access to the higher rebate schedule for general practice [VERIFY: "registration as a specialist satisfies 19AA and you are eligible to access higher Medicare rebates for your specialty"]
- stop needing supervision as a condition of registration, because supervision is a requirement of limited and provisional registration, and fellowship takes you to specialist registration [VERIFY: Medical Board supervision page: supervision remains a requirement for the duration of limited or provisional registration]

What it does not do is let you move to a non-DPA practice on the strength of the fellowship alone.

## What does end or shorten the moratorium

Three things, according to the Department's moratorium page. [VERIFY: each point below against the page]

1. **Time.** For a permanent resident or citizen, the location restrictions end after ten years from first Australian medical registration. For a temporary resident they continue unchanged. Residency status is the hinge, not fellowship.
2. **Scaling.** Working in a more remote location earns scaling credits that bring forward the date you can work outside a DPA. The Department publishes a table by remoteness area: inner regional reduces the period to about nine years, outer regional to about seven, remote to about six and very remote to about five, provided a monthly Medicare billing threshold is met. Scaling is applied automatically by Medicare once billing data shows the threshold has been reached. [VERIFY: the current scaling table and the billing threshold on the moratorium page]
3. **A section 19AB exemption for a specific location.** Exemptions exist, but they are limited and location-specific. They do not end the moratorium; they let you bill at one place while it runs. [VERIFY: exemptions page]

## A note on the 5-year Overseas Trained Doctor Scheme

Participants on this scheme are not eligible for scaling credits, because the scheme already offers its own moratorium reduction. If you were on it, your position is different and worth checking separately. [VERIFY: wording about the 5-year OTD Scheme on the moratorium page]

## How to check where you stand

You can see your current scaling credits, the date you become eligible for the class exemption from section 19AB, and the end date of your moratorium through your Health Professional Online Services (HPOS) account. The Department says it does not have access to that information, and neither do we. [VERIFY: "Checking your scaling status" section]

If the HPOS date and your own understanding disagree, that is the moment to get advice before signing a contract, not after. Tell us your date of first registration, your residency status and where you want to work, and we will tell you what applies.

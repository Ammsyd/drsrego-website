If you employ, or want to employ, an internationally trained GP who is subject to section 19AB, one fact about your practice matters more than any other: whether its location is a Distribution Priority Area. Get it wrong and the doctor cannot get a Medicare provider number for your practice, however good the fit. This guide shows you how to check it yourself, in about five minutes.

## What DPA is, in one paragraph

Distribution Priority Area is a classification the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing applies to GP catchments where the level of GP services is below a benchmark. Doctors restricted by section 19AB (the 10-year moratorium) generally need to work in a DPA location to access Medicare benefits. The classification is reviewed each year. [VERIFY: annual update and the benchmark description on the DPA page]

## Why your street address, not your suburb

DPA is set across 824 GP catchments that the Department draws using patient flows, population, workforce and geography. Catchments do not follow suburb or postcode boundaries. Two practices in the same suburb can sit in different catchments, and a catchment boundary can run down a road. [VERIFY: number of catchments and the boundary factors on the DPA page]

That is why we ask for a street address on our request form, and why a check done on a suburb name is not good enough.

## The three automatic rules worth knowing

Before you look anything up, the DPA page sets out rules that decide many locations outright. [VERIFY: each rule below]

- Areas classified MM 2 to MM 7 under the Modified Monash Model are DPA.
- Inner metropolitan MM 1 areas are not DPA.
- The whole of the Northern Territory and Tasmania are DPA.

Everything else in MM 1 is assessed against the benchmark each year. That is the group where outer-metropolitan practices sometimes qualify and sometimes lose the classification.

## Step by step: the Health Workforce Locator

1. Open the Health Workforce Locator on health.gov.au (the link is in the sources below). [VERIFY: current URL of the tool itself, not just the information page]
2. Enter the practice's full street address, including the postcode.
3. Read off the classifications for that address. The Locator shows Distribution Priority Area, District of Workforce Shortage, Modified Monash Model and the remoteness classifications. [VERIFY: list of classifications shown]
4. Record three things: the DPA status (yes or no), the MMM category (MM 1 to MM 7), and today's date. The date matters because the classification can change at the annual update.
5. If the Locator shows a result you did not expect, check that the address resolved to the right point on the map before relying on it.

## Why MMM matters alongside DPA

The Modified Monash Model measures remoteness and town size on a scale from MM 1 (major cities) to MM 7 (very remote). It is updated after each Census; the current dataset is the 2023 one. [VERIFY: "current 2023 MMM classifications" on the MMM page]

MMM matters to a practice for two reasons. First, MM 2 to MM 7 locations are automatically DPA, so the MMM category often settles the DPA question. Second, a number of workforce programmes and incentives use MMM to define eligibility, so it affects what support is available to a doctor working with you. [VERIFY: programmes that use MMM for eligibility]

## How often it changes

The Department updates DPA annually. The most recent update on its page at the time of writing was March 2025. [VERIFY: date of the latest annual DPA update] A practice can also ask for a review of its catchment's DPA status if exceptional circumstances are affecting access to services. A status granted on review lasts until the next annual update. [VERIFY: the exceptional circumstances review process on the Locator page]

The practical consequence: a check is only as good as its date. If you checked in February, check again after the annual update before you sign anything.

## What we do before we shortlist

Every practice that asks us for a doctor gets its street address checked on the Locator, the result recorded with the date, and the doctor's own section 19AB position assessed against it. Every advert on our jobs board shows the MMM category, the DPA status and the date we checked. If a classification changes, the advert changes.

If you would rather we did the check and put the result in writing as part of a wider readiness audit, that is the IMG-Readiness Audit. If you already know you are DPA and want a doctor, start with the request form.

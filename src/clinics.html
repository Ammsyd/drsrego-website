Where clinics get stuck

Three failure points we see repeatedly.

  1. Supervision capacity discovered too late

    The employment contract is signed. Then the practice discovers no eligible supervisor is available, and the start date slips indefinitely.

  2. Provider-number delays that cost billable weeks

    The doctor has arrived and cannot bill. Every week waiting on a 19AB exemption or provider number is revenue the practice never recovers.

  3. Lapsed supervision paperwork

    A renewal or progress report slips past its date unnoticed, and a routine obligation becomes a compliance problem involving the Board.

Common questions

Common questions about employing an IMG.

Can our practice employ an overseas-trained doctor?

Most accredited general practices can, provided three things line up: the practice location supports the doctor’s Medicare access (for a doctor subject to section 19AB, usually a Distribution Priority Area), an approved supervisor is available, and the doctor holds or can obtain the right AHPRA registration for the role. Our IMG-readiness audit checks all three in writing before you commit.

Source: {{ regulatory.s19ab.short }}, last verified {{ regulatory.s19ab.verified | dateAU }}.

What is a DPA?

A Distribution Priority Area (DPA) is a classification used by the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing to identify GP catchments with lower access to GP services than a benchmark. Doctors subject to section 19AB generally need to work in a DPA location to access Medicare benefits during their moratorium. DPA applies to general practice; the older District of Workforce Shortage (DWS) classification still applies to non-GP specialists. DPA is assessed by GP catchment and reviewed annually, and it draws on the Modified Monash Model (MMM) remoteness classification.

Source: {{ regulatory.dpa.short }}, last verified {{ regulatory.dpa.verified | dateAU }}.

Who can supervise an IMG?

A supervisor must be approved by the Medical Board of Australia, and every IMG holding limited or provisional registration must be supervised for as long as that registration lasts. The Board’s supervised practice guidelines set out who can supervise and at what level; in general practice this is commonly an experienced, vocationally registered GP with enough capacity at your practice for the level required. Not every senior doctor qualifies, which is why we assess supervision capacity before anything is signed.

Source: {{ regulatory.supervision.short }}, last verified {{ regulatory.supervision.verified | dateAU }}.

Do we need to be in a rural area to employ an IMG?

Not necessarily. DPA is assessed by GP catchment, not by suburb. Areas classified MM 2 to MM 7 under the Modified Monash Model are automatically DPA, inner metropolitan MM 1 areas are not, and other MM 1 catchments are assessed against a billing benchmark each year, so some outer-metropolitan catchments qualify. We check both classifications for your exact street address as part of the readiness audit.
